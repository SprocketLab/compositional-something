# Research Plan: SCAN-CSI

## Low-compute compositional self-improvement for language-to-action sequence transduction

**Primary dataset:** SCAN, with the official length split as the main experiment and the add-primitive/template splits as optional diagnostics.

**Core curriculum:** action-sequence length 1–8 → 9–14 → 15–22, followed by direct evaluation on the untouched official 24–48-action length test set.

**Intended role in the paper:** a controlled mechanism check, not the headline real-world result.

**Compute profile:** very low. Inputs are at most a few words, outputs are at most 48 action tokens, composition is deterministic, and no external verifier or environment is required.

---

## 1. Executive summary

This study tests whether compositional self-improvement can teach a small sequence model to directly execute longer SCAN commands after receiving labels constructed from its own predictions on shorter subcommands.

SCAN commands have an explicit grammar. A command such as:

```text
jump opposite left and walk thrice
```

has two children:

```text
jump opposite left  ->  LTURN LTURN JUMP
walk thrice         ->  WALK WALK WALK
```

and the parent output is obtained by concatenation. Other operators repeat, reverse, or surround a child output with turn actions. This gives an exact composition algebra over model-predicted child sequences.

The primary experiment hides the gold labels of progressively longer commands. The seed model is trained only on commands whose target contains at most eight actions. At round 1, it predicts shorter pieces of 9–14-action commands, composes those predictions according to the parse tree, and retrains on accepted parent examples. Round 2 repeats the process for 15–22-action commands. The resulting direct model is evaluated on the official SCAN length test set, whose outputs contain 24–48 actions.

The essential comparison is:

1. **Direct pseudo-labeling:** label each longer command in one generation.
2. **Unfiltered composition:** compose all child predictions.
3. **Guarded composition:** reject malformed or structurally inconsistent child predictions before composition.
4. **Frozen recursive execution:** keep recursively composing the seed model at inference, without retraining.
5. **Gold-supervised frontier:** train on the hidden true labels as an upper-bound diagnostic.

Because SCAN labels are generated by a known symbolic grammar, this experiment cannot support a claim that human annotation is intrinsically unavailable. Its scientific value is narrower and important: it provides the cheapest setting in which to verify the paper's iterative `decompose → predict → compose → retrain` mechanism, test the frozen-composition baseline, and measure error accumulation as composition depth grows.

---

## 2. Research question

> Can iterative retraining on action sequences composed from reliable shorter predictions expand a small model's direct execution-length frontier more effectively than direct self-training or permanent recursive execution?

A convincing result should establish that:

- composed frontier labels are more accurate than direct frontier predictions;
- retraining after each band improves direct execution of longer commands;
- the final direct model outperforms the seed model on the untouched 24–48-action test set;
- iterative retraining improves over recursively composing the original seed model forever;
- one-command and short-command performance is preserved through replay;
- gains are not limited to one operator such as `and`.

---

## 3. Dataset facts and experimental bands

The official SCAN repository contains 20,910 command/action-sequence pairs and several standard compositional splits. The official length split contains 16,990 training commands with outputs of length 1–22 and 3,920 test commands with outputs of length 24–48.

The following counts come directly from the official length-split files and should be recomputed in the data-preparation script as an integrity check:

| Band | Official file | Number of commands | Use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1–8 actions | length train | 6,268 | labeled seed pool |
| 9–14 actions | length train | 6,882 | round-1 unlabeled frontier pool |
| 15–22 actions | length train | 3,840 | round-2 unlabeled frontier pool |
| 24–32 actions | length test | 3,216 | untouched final evaluation |
| 33–48 actions | length test | 704 | hardest untouched evaluation |

Do not pseudo-label or train on the official length test file in the main experiment.

## 4. What is composed

Let `parse(x)` be the SCAN derivation tree for command `x`. Each node is either a primitive, a unary modifier, or a binary connective.

### 4.1 Primitive predictions

The model predicts action sequences for primitive or currently in-frontier subcommands:

```text
walk        -> WALK
jump left   -> LTURN JUMP
run twice   -> RUN RUN
```

The method must not look up the hidden gold output of the parent command.

### 4.2 Exact structural operators

For child output sequences `a` and `b`:

```text
x and y       -> a || b
x after y     -> b || a
x twice       -> a || a
x thrice      -> a || a || a
```

Directional operators apply fixed turn actions around the predicted primitive action:

```text
x opposite left   -> LTURN LTURN || a
x opposite right  -> RTURN RTURN || a
x around left     -> (LTURN || a) repeated four times
x around right    -> (RTURN || a) repeated four times
```

Here `||` denotes sequence concatenation.

### 4.3 Parent pseudo-label

For a parent parse node `v` with children `c_1, ..., c_m`:

\[
\tilde y_v = C_v\big(\hat f(c_1),\ldots,\hat f(c_m)\big),
\]

where `C_v` is the SCAN operator associated with `v`.

The full command receives `\tilde y_root` as its pseudo-label only if every required child prediction and every structural check passes.

## 5. Scientific caveat and positioning

SCAN is unusually favorable because the grammar is fully known and a symbolic interpreter can generate every target exactly. Therefore:

- do not present SCAN as evidence that CSI replaces unavailable annotation;
- do present it as a controlled test of the learning dynamics;
- report a symbolic interpreter as a sanity-check oracle, not as a learning baseline;
- make the frozen recursive-composition comparison central;
- place SCAN in the main paper only if it provides a particularly clean mechanistic figure; otherwise place it in an appendix.

A reviewer should be able to see that the experiment asks whether **recycling composed predictions into the model** has value beyond simply running the known interpreter or recursively calling the model.

## 6. Data acquisition and reproducibility

1. Download the official SCAN repository.
2. Pin the repository commit hash.
3. Preserve the raw files unchanged.
4. Parse every line of the form:

   ```text
   IN: <command> OUT: <action sequence>
   ```

5. Recompute file counts, minimum/maximum output lengths, and checksums.
6. Store a manifest with:
   - source file;
   - line number;
   - canonical command;
   - hidden gold actions;
   - parse tree;
   - operator signature;
   - action length;
   - split assignment.

Use the hidden gold actions only for evaluation and pseudo-label auditing. They must never be read by the pseudo-label generator or acceptance guard.

---

## 7. Parser and canonical representation

Implement a deterministic parser for the official grammar. A practical node schema is:

```json
{
  "node_type": "and",
  "surface": "jump opposite left and walk thrice",
  "children": [
    {"node_type": "opposite_left", "surface": "jump opposite left"},
    {"node_type": "thrice", "surface": "walk thrice"}
  ]
}
```

Every parsed node should store:

- its surface subcommand;
- operator type;
- child nodes;
- a structural action-count estimate derived from the grammar;
- a canonical operator signature, such as `AND(OPPOSITE_L(JUMP), THRICE(WALK))`.

The parser must round-trip all 20,910 official commands. Any command that fails parsing is a data-pipeline error and should stop the run.

---

## 8. Frontier-aware decomposition

The decomposition should use the largest subcommands that remain within the previous reliable frontier rather than always reducing to primitives.

For a current frontier limit `L_t`, recursively cut a target parse tree as follows:

```text
cut(node, L_t):
    if structural_length(node) <= L_t:
        return PREDICT(node)
    otherwise:
        recursively cut the child nodes
        compose their predictions with node.operator
```

This creates a meaningful distinction between:

- **frozen primitive execution**, which recursively uses the seed model at all depths;
- **iterative CSI**, which can use the newly trained round-1 model to solve larger subtrees as single components in round 2.

### Proposed limits

| Stage | Directly predicted component limit | Parent pool |
|---|---:|---:|
| Seed | 1–8 actions | labeled seed examples |
| Round 1 | ≤8 actions | 9–14 actions |
| Round 2 | ≤14 actions | 15–22 actions |
| Final test | direct model only | 24–48 actions |

For the frozen recursive baseline, always use the original seed checkpoint for all predicted leaves or subtrees.

---

## 9. Guarded composition

The SCAN composition rule has no semantic residual error when the child outputs are correct. The guard therefore targets malformed and internally inconsistent predictions rather than boundary interactions.

### 9.1 Required checks

Accept a child prediction only when:

- every token belongs to the official action alphabet;
- there is at least one action token;
- no explanatory prose, punctuation, or duplicate serialization marker is present;
- the output terminates normally within the decoding limit;
- the action count satisfies operator-derived lower and upper bounds;
- applying the parent operator does not exceed the configured maximum output length;
- repeated calls on deterministic prompts do not disagree, if an agreement guard is enabled.

### 9.2 Optional structural checks

For unary operators, check algebraic invariants:

- `twice(x)` must consist of two identical predicted child blocks after composition;
- `thrice(x)` must consist of three identical blocks;
- `opposite` must add exactly two turns of the specified direction;
- `around` must add four copies of the same turn-plus-child block.

These checks inspect the composition result, not the hidden gold label.

### 9.3 Guard variants

Run at least three variants:

1. **No guard:** concatenate every decoded child output.
2. **Syntax guard:** action alphabet and serialization checks only.
3. **Full structural guard:** syntax plus operator invariants and optional agreement.

The expectation is that SCAN's residual rule error is near zero, so the guard may matter less than in addition. That is a useful negative or boundary result.

---

## 10. Split construction

### 10.1 Seed and frontier pools

Within each official length-train band, create deterministic hash-based internal splits:

- 80% pseudo-label/training pool;
- 10% validation/audit pool;
- 10% internal held-out test pool.

Keep all identical canonical commands in one split. Because SCAN has no entities or documents, command-level hashing is sufficient.

### 10.2 Suggested low-compute sample sizes

The full bands are small enough to use, but a minimum experiment can use:

| Stage | Gold/pseudo training examples | Validation | Internal test |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Seed 1–8 | 2,000–4,000 gold | 500 | 500 |
| Round 1, 9–14 | 2,000 accepted | 500 | 500 |
| Round 2, 15–22 | 2,000 accepted | 400 | 400 |
| Official final | none | none | all 3,920 |

Sample uniformly across action length and operator signature, not uniformly across raw rows. Otherwise common templates dominate.

### 10.3 Leakage rules

- Never use official length-test labels for threshold tuning.
- Never use hidden frontier labels to decide whether an individual pseudo-label is accepted.
- Gold labels may be used only after a pseudo-label file is frozen, to compute its precision.
- Tune guard thresholds on seed-validation examples or a separate labeled diagnostic subset.

---

## 11. Iterative training procedure

### Seed model

Train on labeled 1–8-action commands. Use substantial operator balance so the seed sees every primitive and composition operator.

### Round 1

1. Take an unlabeled 9–14-action command.
2. Parse it.
3. Cut it into components of structural length at most eight.
4. Predict each component with the seed model.
5. Compose the predictions through the parse tree.
6. Apply the guard.
7. Add accepted `(full command, composed sequence)` pairs to the round-1 set.
8. Fine-tune from the seed checkpoint on:
   - round-1 pseudo-labels;
   - seed replay;
   - optional component-format replay.

### Round 2

Repeat on 15–22-action commands, but allow the round-1 model to predict subcommands of length at most 14.

### Final evaluation

Run the final model directly on every untouched official length-test command. Do not expose the parse tree or use recursive execution for the primary score.

---

## 12. Training configuration

A small encoder-decoder or decoder-only model is sufficient.

Recommended constraints:

- 60M–1B parameters;
- maximum input length below 64 tokens;
- maximum output length 64 action tokens;
- greedy decoding for pseudo-label generation;
- one to three epochs per stage;
- seed replay ratio between 30% and 50%;
- early stopping on internal frontier validation exact match;
- one development seed, then three random seeds for the final comparison.

A full-parameter T5-small run is likely cheaper than adapter training on a larger model. If consistency with the rest of the paper matters, use the same sub-billion-parameter instruction model and LoRA configuration as the other tasks.

---

## 13. Baselines

### B0 — Seed-only direct model

Train only on 1–8-action gold examples and evaluate directly at all lengths.

### B1 — Direct pseudo-labeling

At each round, ask the current model to predict the full frontier command in one generation. Apply the same output-syntax guard and use the same number of accepted examples as CSI.

### B2 — Unfiltered composition

Compose all component predictions and retrain, without rejection.

### B3 — Guarded composition

The proposed method.

### B4 — Frozen recursive composition

At test time, parse every command and recursively compose predictions from the original seed model. No retraining is performed.

### B5 — Iterative recursive composition without direct training

Use the latest model as a component solver but never train it on full parent commands. This separates better components from learning direct parent execution.

### B6 — Gold-supervised frontier

Train on the hidden gold labels in the same bands and with the same sample counts.

### B7 — Symbolic interpreter

Exact task oracle. Report only as a data/parser sanity check.

---

## 14. Evaluation metrics

### Primary

- exact action-sequence match;
- exact match by target action length;
- largest output length with at least 90% exact match;
- average exact match on the official 24–48-action test set.

### Pseudo-label quality

- exact pseudo-label precision against hidden gold;
- token accuracy;
- guard acceptance rate;
- accepted-example count by operator and length;
- error correlation between child predictions.

### Retention and forgetting

- final accuracy on 1–8 actions;
- final accuracy by primitive;
- final accuracy by operator;
- change from seed checkpoint.

### Efficiency

- generated tokens per accepted pseudo-label;
- training tokens per round;
- direct-model inference calls versus frozen recursive calls;
- wall-clock generation and training time.

## 15. Required analyses

### 15.1 Length-frontier heatmap

Match the visual language of the current paper: rows are output lengths and columns are self-improvement rounds.

### 15.2 Operator slices

Report performance on:

- `and`;
- `after`;
- `twice`;
- `thrice`;
- `opposite`;
- `around`;
- commands combining binary and unary operators.

### 15.3 Frozen-composition error accumulation

Plot accuracy against the number of primitive leaves or model calls. This is the SCAN analogue of the theory's component-error accumulation term.

### 15.4 Filtered-out slice

If the agreement or syntax guard rejects examples, evaluate the final direct model on those same structural slices. Do not overstate this analysis if rejection is dominated by malformed decoding rather than a meaningful semantic interaction.

### 15.5 Optional systematic-transfer runs

After the length experiment is stable, repeat a smaller CSI construction on:

- add-primitive `jump`;
- held-out `around right` template;
- held-out `jump around right` template.

These should be separate experiments with split-specific training. Do not mix their training data into the official length result.

---

## 16. Ablations

Run the following in priority order:

1. with versus without retraining;
2. direct versus composed pseudo-labels;
3. syntax versus full structural guard;
4. primitive-only versus maximal-frontier decomposition;
5. seed replay ratio;
6. 500, 1,000, and 2,000 accepted examples per round;
7. greedy prediction versus two-sample agreement;
8. action-length bands versus parse-depth bands.

The highest-value ablation is primitive-only versus maximal-frontier decomposition. It directly tests whether using the newly expanded model as a larger component solver matters.

---

## 17. Statistical protocol

- Use one fixed data split for all methods.
- Match accepted-example counts across direct and composition baselines.
- Run one seed during development.
- Run three seeds for B0–B4 in the final table.
- Report mean, standard deviation, and paired bootstrap confidence intervals on the official test set.
- Freeze all thresholds before final test evaluation.

---

## 18. Compute budget

A practical minimum run consists of:

- one seed training run;
- two pseudo-label generation passes;
- two short fine-tuning stages;
- four principal baselines;
- one final three-seed confirmation.

Because the complete dataset contains only about 21k examples and sequences are tiny, data processing and evaluation should fit comfortably on a CPU. Training should fit on a single modest GPU, and T5-small may fit on consumer hardware without quantization.

## 19. Risks and mitigations

### Risk 1 — The task is too trivial once the grammar is known

**Mitigation:** make frozen recursive composition and the symbolic interpreter explicit; position the result as a mechanistic control.

### Risk 2 — The seed model is already perfect at 24–48 actions

**Mitigation:** reduce seed examples, seed maximum length, model size, or training epochs until a clear but learnable frontier exists. Pre-register the seed target range, such as 95%+ on 1–8 and below 50% on 24–48.

### Risk 3 — Direct pseudo-labeling is already accurate

**Mitigation:** reduce the seed frontier or use a smaller model. The experiment is informative only when direct frontier labels are measurably noisier than composed labels.

### Risk 4 — Guard has no effect

**Mitigation:** report that composition is exact and the guard is largely unnecessary here. The main comparison remains composition versus direct labeling and retraining versus frozen composition.

### Risk 5 — Round 2 parents cannot be cut into reliable subcommands

**Mitigation:** programmatically verify frontier-composability before training. Report the fraction of each band that admits a cut under the chosen limit.

---

## 20. Go/no-go criteria

Proceed to a full three-seed experiment only if the pilot satisfies:

- seed exact match at least 95% on 1–8 actions;
- seed direct exact match below 70% on 15–22 actions;
- composed round-1 pseudo-label precision at least 95%;
- at least 1,000 accepted round-1 examples;
- round-1 direct model improves by at least five absolute points on the internal 9–14 test band;
- final CSI improves over seed-only direct prediction on the official length test.

Keep SCAN as an appendix-only diagnostic if the symbolic nature makes every model or baseline nearly perfect.

---

## 21. Expected paper claim

A successful result supports the limited claim:

> In a controlled language-to-action domain with exact compositional semantics, iterative retraining on composed predictions expands direct sequence-execution length and can outperform both direct frontier self-training and permanently recursive use of the seed model.

It should not be used alone to support claims about real-world natural-language reasoning.
